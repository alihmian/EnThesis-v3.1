\chapter{Fraïssé Structures and Automorphism Groups}
\label{chap:fraisse-automorphism-groups}

This chapter collects the model-theoretic and permutation-group background used
throughout the thesis.  The first part recalls Fraïssé classes and their
countable ultrahomogeneous limits.  The second passes from a countable structure
to its automorphism group, equipped with the topology of pointwise convergence.
The resulting non-Archimedean Polish groups are the groups whose dynamical and
Ramsey-theoretic properties will be studied in the following chapters.

\section{Structures, ages, and amalgamation}

Let \(L\) be a countable signature, possibly containing relation, function, and
constant symbols.  An \(L\)-structure \(\bfa\) has underlying set
\(A=\dom(\bfa)\), together with an interpretation of every symbol of \(L\).
Throughout, bold capital letters denote structures and the corresponding plain
capitals denote their underlying sets.  An
\emph{embedding} \(f\colon \bfa\hookrightarrow \bfb\) is an injective map that
preserves constants and functions and both preserves and reflects relations.
A bijective embedding is an isomorphism, written \(\bfa\cong \bfb\). When only the
existence of an embedding matters, we write \(\bfa\hookrightarrow \bfb\).

If \(\dom(\bfa)\subseteq\dom(\bfb)\) and the inclusion is an embedding, then \(\bfa\)
is a \emph{substructure} of \(\bfb\), written \(\bfa\leq \bfb\).  For
\(X\subseteq\dom(\bfb)\), the substructure generated by \(X\) is denoted
\(\langle X\rangle_{\bfb}\).  A structure is \emph{finitely generated} if it is
generated by a finite set.  In a relational language this simply means finite;
allowing function symbols is important for examples such as vector spaces and
Boolean algebras.

\begin{definition}[Age]
    The \emph{age} of an \(L\)-structure \(\bfd\), denoted \(\Age(\bfd)\), is the
    class, up to isomorphism, of all finitely generated \(L\)-structures that
    embed into \(\bfd\).
\end{definition}

The three basic closure properties of an age are the following.

\begin{definition}
    Let \(\cK\) be a nonempty class of finitely generated
    \(L\)-structures, closed under isomorphism.
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \(\cK\) has the \emph{hereditary property} \emph{(HP)}
              if \(\bfa\in\cK\) and \(\bfb\leq \bfa\) finitely generated imply
              \(\bfb\in\cK\).
        \item \(\cK\) has the \emph{joint embedding property}
              \emph{(JEP)} if for all \(\bfa,\bfb\in\cK\) there is
              \(\bfc\in\cK\) into which both \(\bfa\) and \(\bfb\) embed.
        \item \(\cK\) has the \emph{amalgamation property} \emph{(AP)}
              if, whenever \(f\colon \bfa\hookrightarrow \bfb\) and
              \(g\colon \bfa\hookrightarrow \bfc\) are embeddings between members
              of \(\cK\), there are \(\bfd\in\cK\) and embeddings
              \(r\colon \bfb\hookrightarrow \bfd\), \(s\colon \bfc\hookrightarrow \bfd\)
              such that \(r\circ f=s\circ g\).
        \item \(\cK\) has the \emph{strong amalgamation property}
              \emph{(SAP)} if the amalgam in \emph{(AP)} can always be chosen
              so that, writing \(A,B,C\) for the underlying sets of
              \(\bfa,\bfb,\bfc\), respectively,
              \[
                  r(B)\cap s(C)=r(f(A)).
              \]
              Thus no points are identified outside the common copy of
              \(\bfa\).
        \item \(\cK\) has the \emph{strong joint embedding property}
              \emph{(SJEP)} if for all \(\bfb,\bfc\in\cK\) there are
              \(\bfd\in\cK\) and embeddings
              \(r\colon\bfb\hookrightarrow\bfd\) and
              \(s\colon\bfc\hookrightarrow\bfd\) such that
              \(r(B)\cap s(C)=\varnothing\).
    \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Every age has \emph{(HP)} and \emph{(JEP)}.  The first property follows by
restricting an embedding into the ambient structure, and the second by taking
the substructure generated by the images of two finitely generated structures.
Amalgamation is the additional condition that makes a coherent homogeneous
limit possible.

\begin{definition}[Fraïssé class]
    A \emph{Fraïssé class} is a nonempty class \(\cK\) of finitely
    generated structures in a countable signature which is closed under
    isomorphism, has countably many isomorphism types, and satisfies
    \emph{(HP)}, \emph{(JEP)}, and \emph{(AP)}.
\end{definition}

In the usual finite-structure setting, arbitrarily large members ensure that
the limit is infinite.  A basic example is the class of finite linear orders.
Its hereditary and joint
embedding properties are immediate, and two finite orders over a common
suborder can be amalgamated by interleaving the remaining points in the
intervals determined by the common part.  Its Fraïssé limit is
\((\Q,<)\).

\section{Fraïssé limits and the extension property}

\begin{definition}
    An \(L\)-structure \(\bfd\) is \emph{ultrahomogeneous} if every isomorphism
    between finitely generated substructures of \(\bfd\) extends to an
    automorphism of \(\bfd\).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Fraïssé]
    \label{thm:fraisse-theorem}
    Let \(\cK\) be a Fraïssé class.  There is a countable
    ultrahomogeneous \(L\)-structure \(\bff\) such that
    \(\Age(\bff)=\cK\), and it is unique up to isomorphism.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}
    The structure in Theorem~\ref{thm:fraisse-theorem} is the
    \emph{Fraïssé limit} of \(\cK\), denoted
    \(\Flim(\cK)\).
\end{definition}

The form of Fraïssé's theorem most useful later is its extension-property
formulation.

\begin{proposition}[Extension property]
    \label{prop:fraisse-extension-property}
    Let \(\bff\) be a countable structure with \(\Age(\bff)=\cK\).  Then \(\bff\)
    is ultrahomogeneous if and only if the following holds:
    whenever \(\bfa\leq \bfb\) belong to \(\cK\) and
    \(f\colon \bfa\hookrightarrow \bff\) is an embedding, there is an embedding
    \(g\colon \bfb\hookrightarrow \bff\) extending \(f\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
    Suppose first that \(\bff\) is ultrahomogeneous.  Embed \(\bfb\) into \(\bff\).
    The given copy of \(\bfa\) and the copy induced by this embedding are
    isomorphic, so ultrahomogeneity supplies an automorphism aligning them.
    Composing with that automorphism gives the required extension of \(f\).

    Conversely, enumerate \(\bff\) and apply the extension property alternately
    to a finite partial isomorphism and its inverse.  The usual back-and-forth
    construction produces an automorphism of \(\bff\) extending the original
    isomorphism between finitely generated substructures.
\end{proof}

For completeness, the construction behind Theorem~\ref{thm:fraisse-theorem}
may be summarised as follows.  Enumerate, up to isomorphism, all triples
\((\bfa,\bfb,f)\) with \(\bfa\leq \bfb\) in \(\cK\) and \(f\) an embedding into a
previous stage.  Repeated amalgamation produces an increasing countable chain
in which every such extension requirement is eventually met.  Its union has
age \(\cK\) and satisfies
Proposition~\ref{prop:fraisse-extension-property}.  Uniqueness follows from
the same back-and-forth argument.  This is the standard proof of Fraïssé's
theorem; see~\cite{Fr}.

The converse will also be used implicitly.

\begin{theorem}
    \label{thm:converse-fraisse}
    If \(\bff\) is a finite or countable ultrahomogeneous structure in a
    countable signature, then \(\Age(\bff)\) is a Fraïssé class.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    The age is countable up to isomorphism and has \emph{(HP)} and
    \emph{(JEP)}.  To verify \emph{(AP)}, embed the common structure
    \(\bfa\) into \(\bff\), and separately embed \(\bfb\) and \(\bfc\) into \(\bff\).
    Ultrahomogeneity allows the latter embeddings to be adjusted by
    automorphisms so that they agree on \(\bfa\).  The substructure generated by
    their two images is a finitely generated member of \(\Age(\bff)\) and is the
    required amalgam.
\end{proof}

\section{Automorphism groups as permutation groups}

Let \(\bfa\) be an \(L\)-structure with domain \(\Omega\).  Its automorphism group
\(\Aut(\bfa)\) is naturally a subgroup of the symmetric group
\(\Sym(\Omega)\). We write \(S_\infty=\Sym(\N)\) for the symmetric
group of the natural numbers with the permutation topology. More generally,
let \(G\leq\Sym(\Omega)\).

For \(X\subseteq\Omega\), the \emph{pointwise stabiliser} and
\emph{setwise stabiliser} are
\[
    G_{(X)}=\{g\in G:g(x)=x\text{ for every }x\in X\},
    \qquad
    G_{\{X\}}=\{g\in G:g(X)=X\}.
\]
For a tuple \(\bar a\in\Omega^n\), its orbit is
\(G\cdot\bar a=\{g\bar a:g\in G\}\).  The action is
\emph{transitive} if it has one orbit on \(\Omega\), while a structure is
\emph{rigid} if its automorphism group is trivial.  In particular, every
finite linear order is rigid, a fact that will be important for Ramsey order
classes.

\section{The permutation topology}

For tuples \(\bar a,\bar b\in\Omega^n\), put
\[
    S(\bar a,\bar b)
    =\{g\in\Sym(\Omega):g\bar a=\bar b\}.
\]
These sets form a basis for the \emph{permutation topology}, equivalently the
topology of pointwise convergence.  Every subgroup of \(\Sym(\Omega)\) is
given the induced topology.

\begin{proposition}
    \label{prop:permutation-topology}
    Let \(G\leq\Sym(\Omega)\).
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \(G\), with the permutation topology, is a topological group.
        \item The pointwise stabilisers \(G_{(F)}\), for finite
              \(F\subseteq\Omega\), form a neighbourhood basis at the
              identity.
        \item A subgroup \(H\leq G\) is open if and only if it contains
              \(G_{(F)}\) for some finite \(F\subseteq\Omega\).
        \item A subgroup \(H\leq G\) is dense in \(G\) if and only if \(H\)
              and \(G\) have the same orbits on \(\Omega^n\) for every
              \(n\geq1\).
    \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    Agreement on finitely many coordinates is preserved under sufficiently
    small changes of either factor in a product, and inversion exchanges
    \(S(\bar a,\bar b)\) with \(S(\bar b,\bar a)\); hence the group operations
    are continuous.  A basic neighbourhood of the identity requires a
    permutation to fix a finite tuple, which proves the second and third
    assertions.  Finally, a basic neighbourhood of \(g\in G\) meets \(H\)
    exactly when, for the relevant finite tuple \(\bar a\), some
    \(h\in H\) satisfies \(h\bar a=g\bar a\).  This is precisely equality of
    the finite-tuple orbits.
\end{proof}

\section{Closed groups and canonical structures}

The closed subgroups of a symmetric group are exactly the automorphism groups
of relational structures.

\begin{theorem}
    \label{thm:closed-subgroups-automorphism-groups}
    A subgroup \(G\leq\Sym(\Omega)\) is closed in the permutation topology if
    and only if there is a relational structure \(\bfa\) on \(\Omega\) such that
    \(G=\Aut(\bfa)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    If \(\bfa\) is a structure on \(\Omega\) and \(g\notin\Aut(\bfa)\), then \(g\)
    fails to preserve some atomic relation on a finite tuple.  Every
    permutation agreeing with \(g\) on that tuple has the same failure, so the
    complement of \(\Aut(\bfa)\) is open.

    Conversely, suppose \(G\) is closed.  For every \(n<\omega\) and every
    \(G\)-orbit \(\Delta\subseteq\Omega^n\), introduce an \(n\)-ary relation
    \(R_\Delta\), interpreted as \(\Delta\).  Let \(\bfa_G\) be the resulting
    relational structure.  Certainly \(G\leq\Aut(\bfa_G)\).  If
    \(h\in\Aut(\bfa_G)\) and \(\bar a\) is a finite tuple, then \(h\bar a\)
    belongs to the same \(G\)-orbit as \(\bar a\).  Thus some \(g\in G\)
    agrees with \(h\) on \(\bar a\), so every basic neighbourhood of \(h\)
    meets \(G\).  Closedness gives \(h\in G\), proving
    \(\Aut(\bfa_G)=G\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
    \label{cor:automorphism-groups-are-closed}
    For every structure \(\bfa\), the group \(\Aut(\bfa)\) is closed in
    \(\Sym(\dom(\bfa))\).
\end{corollary}

The structure \(\bfa_G\) in the proof is the \emph{canonical structure} of \(G\).
It contains exactly the finite-orbit information of the action.  It also has
the homogeneity required by Fraïssé theory.

\begin{proposition}
    \label{prop:canonical-structure-ultrahomogeneous}
    If \(G\leq\Sym(\Omega)\) is closed, then its canonical structure \(\bfa_G\)
    is ultrahomogeneous and \(\Aut(\bfa_G)=G\).  If \(\Omega\) is countable, the
    signature of \(\bfa_G\) is countable.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    Only ultrahomogeneity remains to be checked.  Let
    \(p\colon \bfb\hookrightarrow \bfc\) be an isomorphism between finite substructures of
    \(\bfa_G\), and enumerate the underlying set \(B\) without repetition as \(\bar b\).  The tuple
    \(\bar b\) satisfies the relation naming its own \(G\)-orbit.  Since \(p\)
    preserves that relation, \(p\bar b\in G\cdot\bar b\).  Some element of
    \(G\) therefore agrees with \(p\) on \(B\), and hence extends \(p\).
    When \(\Omega\) is countable, each \(\Omega^n\) is countable, so only
    countably many orbit relations are introduced.
\end{proof}

\section{The Polish topology}

A topological space is \emph{Polish} if it is separable and admits a compatible
complete metric.  A topological group is Polish when its underlying space is
Polish.

\begin{theorem}
    \label{thm:aut-countable-polish}
    If \(\bfa\) has countable domain, then \(\Aut(\bfa)\), with the permutation
    topology, is a Polish group.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Let \(\Omega=\dom(\bfa)\).  The space \(\Omega^\Omega\), with \(\Omega\)
    discrete, is Polish.  The map
    \[
        \Sym(\Omega)\longrightarrow\Omega^\Omega\times\Omega^\Omega,
        \qquad g\longmapsto(g,g^{-1}),
    \]
    identifies \(\Sym(\Omega)\) with the closed set of pairs \((f,h)\)
    satisfying \(f\circ h=h\circ f=\id_\Omega\).  Hence
    \(\Sym(\Omega)\) is Polish.  By
    Corollary~\ref{cor:automorphism-groups-are-closed}, \(\Aut(\bfa)\) is a
    closed subgroup, and closed subgroups of Polish groups are Polish.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
    \label{prop:countable-local-basis-open-subgroups}
    If \(\Omega\) is countable and \(G\leq\Sym(\Omega)\), then
    \[
        \{G_{(F)}:F\subseteq\Omega\text{ finite}\}
    \]
    is a countable neighbourhood basis at the identity consisting of open
    subgroups.
\end{proposition}

Thus automorphism groups of countable structures are
\emph{non-Archimedean Polish groups}.  The finite-stabiliser basis is the
bridge between their topology and finite structural information.  In the next
chapter these groups act on compact spaces, and the resulting fixed-point
properties will be translated into Ramsey properties of their ages.
